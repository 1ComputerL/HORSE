Horse Care & Training

Master the essentials of keeping horses healthy, happy, and well-trained

Daily Horse Care

Proper horse care is essential for maintaining health, happiness, and longevity. A daily routine establishes good habits for both horse and caretaker.

Daily Care Checklist

Morning Routine

  • Check horse for injuries, lameness, or illness
  • Clean water buckets and provide fresh water
  • Provide morning hay or pasture access
  • Feed grain (if appropriate for the horse)
  • Muck out stalls completely
  • Check hooves for debris or injuries

Throughout the Day

  • Ensure constant access to fresh water
  • Monitor pasture condition and safety
  • Check on horse multiple times
  • Provide hay between meals

Evening Routine

  • Final health check
  • Clean water and provide fresh water
  • Provide evening hay
  • Muck out stalls
  • Check hooves again before bedding down
  • Secure gates and check fences

Nutrition & Feeding

Horses are herbivores and grazers by nature. A proper diet is fundamental to their health.

Basic Dietary Requirements

  • Hay/Pasture: 50-100% of daily caloric intake; minimum 1.5% of body weight daily
  • Fresh Water: 5-10 gallons per day for average horse (more if exercised)
  • Grain: 0.5-1% of body weight daily (adjust based on activity and need)
  • Minerals: Calcium, phosphorus, trace minerals (copper, zinc, selenium)
  • Vitamins: A, D, and E supplementation may be necessary
Feeding Tips:

Feed small meals frequently (3-4 times daily) rather than large meals once or twice daily. This mimics natural grazing patterns and aids digestion.

Grooming

Regular grooming is essential for health, hygiene, and bonding with your horse.

Basic Grooming Steps

  • Curry Comb: Use circular motions to loosen dirt and dead hair
  • Dandy Brush: Flick away loose hair and dirt
  • Body Brush: Smooth the coat for a shiny appearance
  • Mane and Tail Comb: Gently detangle to avoid hair loss
  • Face Cloth: Gently clean face, eyes, and nostrils
  • Hoof Pick: Remove debris from hooves daily

Hoof Care

A horse's feet require regular attention. "No foot, no horse" is a well-known equestrian saying.

Hoof Care Essentials

  • Daily Picking: Pick hooves before and after rides to remove debris
  • Regular Trimming: Every 6-8 weeks by a qualified farrier
  • Shoeing: Every 6-8 weeks if the horse is shod
  • Inspections: Regular farrier checks for issues
  • Hoof Hygiene: Keep stalls clean and dry to prevent thrush

Health & Veterinary Care

Annual Veterinary Visits

  • Physical examination
  • Vaccinations (against tetanus, equine influenza, etc.)
  • Dental examination and floating teeth
  • Deworming
  • Discussion of health concerns

Common Health Issues

  • Colic: Digestive upset; requires immediate attention
  • Lameness: Limping or uneven gait; may indicate pain
  • Thrush: Fungal/bacterial infection in hooves
  • Laminitis: Inflammatory condition of the hooves
  • Wounds: Require proper cleaning and care

Training Basics

Training should be based on positive reinforcement, consistency, and understanding of equine behavior.

Training Principles

  • Be Consistent: Use the same cues for the same responses
  • Reward Good Behavior: Use treats, praise, or release of pressure
  • Stay Calm: Horses are sensitive to your emotions
  • Start Simple: Build foundation skills before advanced techniques
  • Be Patient: Each horse learns at their own pace
  • Safety First: Always prioritize safety for you and the horse

Training Stages

  • Groundwork: Leading, longing, lunging, desensitization
  • Backing: Introduction to saddle and rider
  • Basic Riding: Walk, trot, canter, stopping
  • Advanced Training: Collection, lateral movements, jumping (as desired)
Professional Help:

Consider working with a qualified trainer, especially for starting young horses or if you're new to horse care and training. Professional guidance can prevent bad habits and injuries.

Exercise & Turnout

  • Horses need at least 1-2 hours of pasture time daily
  • Regular exercise prevents behavioral issues and maintains health
  • Vary exercise to prevent boredom and overuse injuries
  • Start slow in spring and increase gradually
  • Cool down properly after exercise

Shelter & Environment

  • Shelter: Protection from extreme weather
  • Pasture: Safe, well-maintained grazing areas
  • Fencing: Secure 4.5-5 feet tall
  • Water: Clean, accessible water sources
  • Space: Minimum 1 acre per horse (more is better)
